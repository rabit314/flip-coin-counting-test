Coin Flip turns an abstract idea into something you can see, play with, and question. Because sometimes the best way to understand probability is to let it happen.

Meet your probability playground.

The idea is simple: 1,000 people each flip a coin. Some get heads. Some get tails. You choose who stays, flip again, and watch a crowd become a handful.

Built for students, educators, and anyone with a curious streak, this interactive experiment explores randomness, independent events, and the difference between an expected outcome and an actual one.

Every dot is a possibility.

Each dot represents one person. An unflipped coin is neutral, heads are orange and marked H, and tails are green and marked T. The counters, distribution bar, and move history help you follow how the experiment changes.

Coins are simulated independently in your browser using JavaScript's Math.random(). This is an educational simulation, rather than a source of cryptographic randomness.

Created by Rabit Bin Khalil, with assistance from ChatGPT. A small web project built to make probability a little more approachable.

A THOUGHT TO TAKE WITH YOU

Chance has no memory.
